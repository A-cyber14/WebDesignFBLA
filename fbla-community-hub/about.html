About Pinellas Connect

One trusted place to find help in Pinellas.

Our mission is to make every essential Pinellas County resource easy to find, easy to understand, and easy to trust, for every resident.

The problem we solve

Pinellas County has strong safety-net services, but information about them is spread across dozens of county pages, nonprofit websites, PDFs, and social posts. Someone facing an eviction, a lost job, or a hurricane evacuation shouldn't have to search all of them.

Pinellas Connect brings verified food, housing, health, job, transportation, senior, disability, family, veteran, financial, and emergency resources into one searchable, mobile-friendly site with a map. When our directory can't answer a question, we point people to 211 (First Contact), which helps 24 hours a day.

Who we serve

  • Families looking for food, child care, and school resources
  • Seniors and caregivers who benefit from larger text and simple navigation
  • Job seekers and students searching for training and work
  • Veterans navigating benefits and health care
  • New residents learning what the county offers
  • Caseworkers and volunteers who refer others to services

How we verify information

People may rely on this site for essential needs, so accuracy came before everything else.

  • Official sources only

    Each listing was checked against the organization's own website or an official government page (pinellas.gov, floridahealth.gov, va.gov, myflfamilies.com). Directory and review sites were never used as the source of truth.

  • Nothing invented

    If a source didn't state hours, costs, eligibility, or a service area, the listing says “Not listed.” Where sources disagreed (for example, PSTA's two fare tables), we say so and send people to the source.

  • Real coordinates

    Map pins come from geocoding each verified street address with OpenStreetMap Nominatim or the U.S. Census Bureau Geocoder. Places that couldn't be matched to a building aren't mapped.

All listings were last verified on . Services change, so please confirm with the organization before visiting. Events come from official calendars and are hidden automatically after they end.

How we planned and built the site

  1. 1. Research

    Identified Pinellas County's most-needed service categories and the official agencies behind them.

  2. 2. Verify

    Checked every organization against official sources and recorded the date and source page.

  3. 3. Plan

    Mapped a sitemap of eight pages and sketched phone-first wireframes, including a dedicated hurricane page.

  4. 4. Design & build

    Built a design system and wrote the site in plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, with all data in one file.

  5. 5. Test

    Tested on phone, tablet, and desktop, checked color contrast, and used every feature with only a keyboard.

Design decisions

An Apple-inspired approach: clarity first, content over decoration, and depth that helps people understand where they are.

Gulf Coast palette

Deep Gulf blue, sea glass, and warm sand give the site a local identity without looking like a tourism site. Every text color meets the WCAG 2.2 contrast minimum of 4.5:1 [1].

System typography

We use the device's own system font (San Francisco on Apple devices) with Inter as a fallback. Both are clean and highly readable at every size.

Search first

A single search bar with category and location sits at the top of the home page, because people scan the top of a page first [2].

Emergencies are always one tap away

911, 988, and 211 appear on every page, crisis lines are marked in red, and the hurricane page uses large, tappable phone tiles.

Map plus list

The map is visual, but the list view shows the same results for keyboard, screen reader, and offline users.

Free, keyless map

Google Maps requires an API key and a billing account. We used the open-source Leaflet library with OpenStreetMap tiles, which need neither.

Accessibility features

Built to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 [1].

  • “Skip to main content” link and a visible focus ring on every control
  • Every feature works with a keyboard, including map pins and the details dialog
  • “Larger text” button that is remembered on every page
  • A list view for the map, so no information is only visual
  • Labels on every form field, with clear error messages
  • Live result counts announced to screen readers
  • Buttons and links at least 40–48 pixels tall for easy tapping
  • Animations turn off for people who prefer reduced motion
  • Accessible pop-up built with the HTML dialog element [3]
