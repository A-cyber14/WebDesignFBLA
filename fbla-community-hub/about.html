About Riverbend Connect

One place to find community help

Our mission is to make every local resource easy to find, easy to understand, and easy to use — for every resident.

The problem we solve

Communities offer many helpful programs, but the information is often spread across dozens of separate websites, flyers, and social media pages. Residents who need help the most may not know where to start.

Riverbend Connect gathers organizations, programs, and events into one searchable, mobile-friendly directory so residents can find what they need in seconds. When the directory cannot answer a question, we point people to 211, a free referral service that connects people with local help across the United States and Canada [8].

Who we serve

  • 👨‍👩‍👧 Families looking for food, childcare, and youth programs
  • 🧓 Seniors who benefit from larger text and simple navigation
  • 🎓 Students and job seekers searching for training and work
  • 🌎 New residents learning what the community offers
  • 🤝 Volunteers and caseworkers who refer others to services

How we planned and built the site

We followed a five-step design process.

  1. 1. Research

    Identified the target audience and the types of resources they need most, and reviewed accessibility and usability guidelines.

  2. 2. Plan

    Created a sitemap with six pages and sketched wireframes for phone and desktop layouts before writing any code.

  3. 3. Design

    Built a style guide with one color palette, one readable font family, and reusable components such as cards, buttons, and tags.

  4. 4. Develop

    Wrote the site in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript with no frameworks, keeping all resource data in one file that is easy to update.

  5. 5. Test

    Tested on desktop, tablet, and phone screen sizes, checked color contrast, and navigated every page using only a keyboard.

Design decisions and why we made them

Readable font

We use Atkinson Hyperlegible, a free typeface designed by the Braille Institute to make letters easier to tell apart for readers with low vision [3].

Calm, high-contrast colors

Deep teal suggests trust and calm, and warm amber highlights actions. Every text color meets the WCAG 2.2 minimum contrast ratio of 4.5:1 [1], and we checked each color pair with the same contrast formula used by WebAIM's contrast checker [2].

Search first

The search bar sits at the top of the home page because research by Nielsen Norman Group shows that web users often scan pages in an F-shaped pattern, focusing on the top and left [4].

Mobile-first layout

Pew Research Center reports that most U.S. adults own a smartphone [6], so we designed for small screens first and then expanded the layout for tablets and computers.

Consistent cards

Every resource uses the same card layout, which follows the usability principle of “consistency and standards” [5]. Users learn the pattern once and can use it everywhere.

Works offline

All resource data is stored in the site's own files instead of an outside server, so the directory loads quickly and still works if the internet connection is lost.

Accessibility features

We followed the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 [1].

  • “Skip to main content” link for keyboard and screen reader users
  • Every feature works with a keyboard, with a clear focus outline
  • “Larger text” button that is remembered on every page
  • Semantic HTML: header, nav, main, footer, headings, and lists
  • Labels on every form field and clear error messages
  • Live result counts announced to screen readers
  • Buttons and links at least 44 pixels tall for easy tapping
  • Animations turn off for users who prefer reduced motion
  • Decorative images are hidden from screen readers
  • Accessible pop-up built with the HTML dialog element [9]

How we measure success

These key performance indicators (KPIs) show whether residents are actually finding the help they need. The goals below are our own targets for a launch.

Success metrics and goals
MetricWhat it tells usOur goal
Search success rateShare of searches that lead a user to open a resource's details70% or higher
Zero-result search rateSearches that found nothing — shows which resources or keywords are missingUnder 10%
Helpfulness rating“Yes” votes on “Was this information helpful?”80% or higher
ConversionsTaps on phone numbers and saved resources — signs that a user took actionGrow each month
Engagement and bounce rateHow long visitors stay and whether they leave after one pageBounce rate under 40%
Returning visitorsPeople who come back, showing the site is trusted and useful25% of visitors
Core Web VitalsGoogle's page-speed measures: Largest Contentful Paint (LCP), Interaction to Next Paint (INP), and Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) [10]“Good” rating: LCP under 2.5 s, INP under 200 ms, CLS under 0.1
Lighthouse scoresAutomated audits for performance, accessibility, and SEO [11]90 or higher in each category

After launch, we would collect these numbers with a privacy-friendly analytics tool, review them every month, and use A/B testing to try improvements. Feedback from the Contact page would guide which resources to add next.
